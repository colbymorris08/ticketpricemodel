Core hypothesis

When a marquee opponent plays on the road at a new or newly-promoted home venue, secondary-market prices rise more than primary face value — fans pay up for a rare “see them here once” game. Same logic should transfer from WNBA expansion → NWSL-style launches and from PL promoted clubs → new USL/NWSL venues.

Secondary — resale (StubHub, SeatGeek, Viagogo). Reflects scarcity + demand when inventory is tight. Not the same market — we predict secondary, use primary as a feature.

Why this matters for NWSL / new WNBA

  • Expansion teams have no loyalty base yet — opponent brand drives casual demand.
  • Primary prices may stay conservative; secondary tells you true willingness to pay.
  • If PL interaction is stable historically, use it to priors for US league expansion before you have 2+ seasons of data.

Model — planned architecture

Rolling-origin backtest; one row per fixture × horizon (T−30, T−14, T−3).

Features (shared)

  • days_to_event, day-of-week, holiday/weekend
  • opponent_tier, home team “new market” flag
  • opponent_tier × new_market_home
  • Arena capacity, metro population (optional)
  • Primary sold-out proxy, listing count on secondary
  • TV / national broadcast flag

Stack

  • LightGBM — opponent interactions, listing scarcity
  • GAM — smooth calendar / days-to-event curves
  • Ensemble or residual: GAM on time, LGBM on categoricals
  • Metrics: MAPE / RMSE on log(price) at each horizon
